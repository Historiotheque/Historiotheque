# CONSTITUTION

*The doctrine of the incorruptible source.*

*Adopted 2026-09-28 — one constitution for the whole operation, housed at the Source
(the Historiotheque repository root).*

## Preamble — the Source

The Historiotheque repository is the foundation and the home of the Historiotheque
organization. It is the incorruptible Source: what is released stays released, exactly
as it was. The Source governs the satellites; the satellites never govern the Source.

## Part I — Grand Strategy

Grand Strategy is the doctrine of how the Source governs the satellites. The shape of
the Organization is hub-and-spoke: the Source at the center, satellite repositories
branching outward from it, repository-to-repository, each interlinked with the others
and pinned to latest versions.

Doctrine flows outward from the center and never the reverse. One constitution lives
at the Source. Per-repo copies would drift — and drift is corruption of the
incorruptible.

## Part II — The incorruptible source

The source must never be corrupted — not by haste, not by experiment, not by the
operator's own errors. What is released must stay released, exactly as it was. This
is how.

### Article 1 — main is sacred

`main` is always releasable, always clean. No experiment is ever committed to `main`
directly. `main` is the diamond: palatial, crystalline, incorruptible — the NOUS of
the repository.

### Article 2 — branches are workshops

All work happens on branches: one branch per unit of work, branched from `main`,
merged back, deleted. `feature/...`, `fix/...` — small, named, disposable. Branches
are the contained workshop where experiments and failures happen safely. The Invisible
Forces get the workshop; they never touch the crystal.

### Article 3 — merges are the Archivillus moment

Merge with `--no-ff`, so every branch stays visible in the history. The history must
read like a lab notebook: every merge a small dated story of what was tried, what
failed, what was kept. To merge is to collect, log, amend, and fold in.

### Article 4 — versions are immutable

Tag every release (`v1.0`, `v1.1`). A tag is an immutable pointer: the released version
can never silently change under anyone. "The latest version" always resolves to a tag,
never to a moving target.

### Article 5 — review, even alone

Open a pull request for every merge and review the diff fresh — ideally a day later.
The solo dev reviews themselves: adversarial review against one's own operator errors,
before they reach the diamond.

### Article 6 — the gate, not the magic

Incorruptibility is not magic. It is a gate: nothing reaches `main` except through a
branch, a review, and a merge. History is append-only and content-addressed —
tamper-evident, walk-backable. What inspires is the visible discipline: a public
history clean enough to read.
