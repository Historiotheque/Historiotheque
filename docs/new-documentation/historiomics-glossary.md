# Glossary of Historiomics

Version 1.0.0 — 2026-10-08
Placement: Historiotheque repository, `new-documentation/`.

Historiomics is a rigorous framework, not a science and not a theory proper: most of what follows stands at the stage of hypothesis. It began as a fictional construct in the novel *The Archives-Project*, in the mouths of fictional public intellectuals whose art-research project mirror-reflected the author's own art operation, and has been formalized since. It applies at every scale, from an individual to a civilization to the planetary historiosphere; scale is a facet of a case, never a separate theory.

## The framework and its object

**Historiomics** — The framework for studying patterns of health and pathology in historical systems: how histories form, circulate, sicken, and are repaired, within a historiome.

**Historiome** — The total web of historical data, memory, narrative, and material traces belonging to a system (a person, an operation, a society, a civilization), together with the flows that move through it. A historiome has a topology: nodes where flows concentrate, channels, sediments, and holes where communities or records were erased.

**Historiosphere** — The wider sphere in which historiomes circulate and interact; at the limit, the planetary field of historical processes.

**Historiotheque** — The archive-operation: the refinery where historical data is gathered, indexed, kept, and made diagnosable. The operator's own house of practice, in its distributed and mobile forms. (Sites in the field are historiotopes, below — never historiotheques.)

**Historiotope** — A site where historical flows concentrate and where history can be read: a city, an archive, a battlefield, a port, a platform, a ritual place. Every case is anchored in one or more historiotopes.

**Historiotype** — A fundamental unit of historical analysis. Attested kinds: an event, a record, a cultural movement, a historical period.

## Formation and dynamics

**Historiotome** — A structural accretion that grows where flows concentrate in a historiotope: a conduit, a complex, a regime (e.g. a bullion conduit, a fiscal-military state, a plantation complex). Historiotomes are typed by model — energetic, geological, biological, semiotic, organizational. A historiotome is generative in itself; it becomes pathological when it ossifies and couples badly with others.

**Sediment** — What flows leave behind when they harden: charters, ledgers, laws, infrastructures, registries. Sediments constrain and channel later flows; legal and administrative sediments can act as locks on future development.

**Attractor** — A stable regime or pattern a system gravitates toward (an ideology, an economic order, a narrative). **Bifurcation** — the threshold at which a system leaves one attractor for another: revolution, collapse, conversion. **Critical slowing down** — the warning sign of an approaching bifurcation: the system recovers ever more slowly from shocks.

**Feedback loop** — A recursive circuit in which a system's output re-enters as input. Positive loops amplify (repression → radicalization → further repression); negative loops stabilize.

**Dynamical disease** — The core borrowed concept: a pathology in which the machinery of a system is intact, but runaway feedback and bifurcations produce pathological output. Historiopathies are dynamical diseases of historical systems.

**Prison-State (Deterministic Prison-State, DPS)** — A closed formation in which the inhabitants' historical trajectories are fixed in advance by the structure: a system that has lost the capacity to generate new history.

## Pathology

**Historiopathy** — A dynamical disease of a historical system: history blocked, distorted, weaponized, or locked into a self-damaging pattern, arising when feedback loops destabilize attractors or when historiotomes ossify. Diagnosed by signs (objective indicators) and symptoms (lived, collective expressions), with an underlying pathology, a prognosis, and a course (acute, chronic, oscillatory, catastrophic, latent).

**Historiotrauma** — The scar a historiopathy leaves: ruptured continuities, demographic holes, erased languages and records. Transmitted across generations through laws, institutions, registries, and memory practices, and persisting as a defect in the historiome long after the causing events.

**Logopathy** — A disorder of the narrative machinery itself: disordered story-generating processes upstream of historiopathies, which they feed.

**Planetary historiopathies** — Pathological configurations that exceed regional containment. Attested types: extractive; coercive-authoritarian; fragmentation; acceleration; sacralized-mythic; and the composite terminal convergence, in which several activate at once and reinforce one another.

## Dispositions toward history

**Historiophilia** — A love of, and affinity for, history and its manifestation: the non-pathological disposition from which the pathological forms below deviate by excess or direction.

**Historiomania** — Obsessive over-investment in historical narrative to construct identity: a nation thinking of its own history in mythic terms — the "Great Nation," ultranationalism. The Primary Inflation of historical consciousness. Also appears as the compulsion to compile: manic overproduction of historical artifacts and symbols.

**Autohistoriophilia** — The idealization and idolization of one's own history or lineage: a flawless epic of self-justification. The Secondary Inflation, arising from historiomania.

**Historiophobia** — Acute, paralyzing dread of history: the past experienced as an inescapable prison or an unappeasable judge; a retreat from time that paralyzes creative action in the present. With historioclasm, a primary historiopathy, at least in potential.

**Historioclasm** — The destruction of history: the violent breaking, fracturing, or purging of historical linkages — a topological disruption that tears the historical tissue. The behavioral climax of historiophobia: smashing, erasing, or resetting the historical record in a bid for liberation; Year Zero attempts and revolutionary calendar resets are its classic forms.

## Manifestation and reading

**Historiophany** — History manifesting: direct contact with history itself, sudden and complete, like a revelation. Within the framework, a historiophany can precipitate a historiopathy (a revelation that destabilizes an attractor) or mitigate one (a revelation that restores meaning and cohesion). A *false* or *logopathic* historiophany is a manufactured or delusional manifestation — pattern-excess producing a synthetic revelation. A *true* historiophany renders pathology articulable.

**Historiomancy** — Consulting history as an oracular field of signs. Historiophany is the event; historiomancy is the method. "History divined is future discerned."

**Sillage** — The lingering trace a manifestation leaves in consciousness and culture after the event: history's wake.

**Patina, decay, ruin** — The material sign-layer of a historiome: time made visible on surfaces. Patina authenticates and accrues meaning; decay is enrichment as well as loss; the ruin is the endpoint state in which a formation survives as fragments. In diagnostic terms these are indexical signs — the objective evidence from which a historiopathy is read.

**Chronoplasticity** — Time treated as a plastic medium, reshaped across somatic, technical, and historiographical layers. Images, sounds, and words are its levers; when they align in resonance, a historiophany flashes.

## Intervention and outcome

**Historiotherapy (historiotherapeusis; historical therapy)** — Intervention aimed at the underlying dynamics of a historiopathy — its loops, attractors, and suppressed histories — rather than at symptoms alone. It works in two registers: archival and epistemic repair (recovering suppressed records, counter-mapping, pluralizing narratives), and material and legal reparations. Its meta-principles: articulation over erasure; differentiation over compression; circulation over accumulation; integration over acceleration. *Historiotherapeusis* is the term used by Dr. Viktor Kropotkin in the fiction; the words name the same thing.

**Historiovision** — The stabilized interpretive framework that results when manifestations are integrated: the framework, as opposed to the event of manifestation.

**Remission and escalation** — A historiopathy is assessed on five axes. Escalation: blocked flows; rigid historiogrammatical forms; proliferating, unarticulated scars; energetic extremes (hypermobilization with paralysis); temporal compression. Remission runs the other way on each axis: flows reopen without catastrophic rupture; new constructions can hold contradiction; scars are articulated into structural knowledge; energy distributes; temporal layers differentiate again.

**Historiagogy** — The pedagogy of historiophanic experience: teaching a right relationship to history.

## Reserved

**Historiocode** — Reserved for the rare case where no plainer word serves for the generative rules of a historiome.

**Historiogrammatical forms** — The permissible constructions of historical discourse in a period or historiotope: what can be said, sequenced, and legitimated. The adjective is the standing usage.
