# [*Assisted by machine intelligence.] — An Iterative and Experimental Methodology, with Agentic AI

**Version 1.0.0 — 2026-10-08**
Alex Gagnon (A.G.)
The Historiotheque · New Documentation

---

METHODOLOGY:
The methodology begins with the structured integration of decades of artistic research and technological experimentation. This approach now harnesses the power of agentic AI: the work no longer takes place primarily in the exchange of prompts and outputs, but inside a workspace, where the unit of work is a state of that workspace. The instructions are no longer designed for a single session. They are written into standing instruction files — rules, specifications, skills and schemas — deliberated, ratified and versioned, and returned to at every step. The outputs are analyzed critically, identifying elements of novelty and coherence, and subsequent work is crafted to build upon these discoveries. The cycle has widened: it turns across sessions now, from draft to reaction, amendment, verification and publication, each state of the work recorded before the next begins. This recursive refinement transforms the working process into an evolving exploration of creative potential.

A key component of the process involves inducing what are termed productive “hallucinations.” By embedding intricate and occasionally conflicting constraints, the AI generates unexpected and unconventional outputs. These deviations are embraced as sources of serendipity rather than errors, akin to the role of noise in communication systems or randomness in creative exploration. Within the artwork, this remains true. Within the operation, a deviation is treated differently, and the practice has grown a verification layer in response: memory is consulted before acting, divergences from the record are flagged, conflicts between rules are referred to the operator for a ruling, and every change is proved against the record it replaces. Serendipity is curated in the art; it is audited in the operations.

The methodology interweaves this AI-driven exploration with a deep engagement with archival materials and interdisciplinary practices. Personal archives of writings, visual art, and music provide a rich reservoir of inspiration, ensuring a dynamic dialogue between historical creativity and emergent possibilities. The archive has also become a memory architecture: a working surface over a deeper geology of records, in which what lies deeper costs more to raise, and in which the state of the workspace is recorded before it is left, so that no change goes unaccounted between one session and the next. Outputs generated in one medium often inform work in another, creating a networked synergy across disciplines such as painting, digital design, soundscapes, and speculative literature. A change in one work now propagates, deliberately, to everything that depends on it; and a document that outgrows its home is returned upstream, toward its source. This intermedia approach enhances the depth and resonance of the creative outcomes.

The process remains guided by clear creative objectives, ensuring a balance between rigorous design principles and openness to unexpected breakthroughs. Exploratory inquiry, rooted in philosophy and culture, poses challenging and unconventional questions, fostering states of elegant generative tension. These outputs — and now whole states of the work — are subjected to rigorous human ratification. The Chief Art Operator is the fitness function of the whole system: an elegant solution to an aesthetic problem passes, or does not, by his judgment alone. States are ratified before they are published, and versions are assigned at publication, never during drafting. This methodology not only expands the horizons of artistic innovation but also redefines the collaborative potential of human-machine partnerships in the arts: no longer a human consulting a tool from outside the work, but two operators inside one workspace, coordinating through the workspace itself — through the files, logs and records each leaves on its surface for the other.

THE ARCHIVILLUS SYSTEM
In its basic architecture, the system is parsimonious: one operator, one agentic assistant, and a workspace governed by standing instruction files, a memory architecture, and a verification layer. Order within it is kept by precedence, which is both a relation and a function: states precede states, rules precede rules, and given a conflict, precedence returns what governs. Precedence evolves the way common law evolves — by dated rulings that accumulate as precedent, cited by later decisions, distinguished when they are overridden, and never erased.

Proximology, an axiom of Workspace Theory, governs what can be reached: only what is exposed on the surface of the workspace, in proximity, ready to hand, is cheaply callable. What lies in the depths remains accessible, but at a price.

FORMALIZATION
What was described in prose, last year, is now formalized alongside the practice. As of October 2026, the theory of Art Operations has its ontology; Workspace Theory stands at Version 1.0.0; a Procedural Theory and a note on the reproducibility of experiments extend the same discipline to how works are generated and how experiments — reproducible as a class, auditable in the individual case — are documented. The traffic is two-way: the practice generates the theory, and the theory governs the practice.
