# A Note on Reproducibility (of Experiments)

**Version 1.0.0 — 2026-10-08**
Alex Gagnon (A.G.)
The Historiotheque · New Documentation

This note corrects and clarifies what I mean by reproducibility in my experimental art-research practice.

## What I used to claim

I have often said that documentation is what makes my experiments reproducible. At times I stated this without qualification, as if a reader could take an exact procedure from the record and reproduce the work itself. That claim was too broad, and I am correcting it here.

## What I actually do

My work is project-based, serial, iterative, and evolutionary. A project frames the inquiry. A series makes variation comparable. Iterations carry a work through production, review, failure, and revision. Variants branch: some are selected and carried forward, others are abandoned, and some return later as material.

I keep records: logs, versions, releases, notes on decisions and failures. But I do not document every step of a painting or a composition as it happens, and I do not give exact recipes for singular works. A recent test made the first point plain. I tried to note every step while making a digital painting, and I had recorded thirteen steps before anything interesting had even begun.

## Why exhaustive capture fails

Documentation at the granularity of gestures destroys the process it documents. The hand moves faster than the record. Stopping to record changes the state of the work and the state of the operator. The record fills with micro-actions that carry no methodological information, while the decisions that actually shape the work go by unrecorded because the recorder is busy counting movements.

A record of everything is not a method. Exhaustive capture was the wrong standard, and I am replacing it.

## The distinction

Four different claims were collapsed into the single word "reproducible." Separated, they are these:

- **Operation reproducibility.** The Art Operation — its projects, series, methods, records, failures, versions, and teachable starting procedures — can be reconstructed and run by another practitioner. This is the primary claim.
- **Experimental reproducibility.** For a given experiment, the record states the question, the materials and tools in general terms, the starting conditions, the phases in order, the critical parameters or parameter ranges, the decision rules applied, the deviations, the failures, and the outcome. That is enough to run the same kind of experiment and compare the class of result.
- **Artifact non-cloning.** A singular painting, composition, or text is not promised as duplicable. An attempted duplicate changes category — study, copy, performance, cover, or forgery — depending on the claim made for it.
- **Auditability.** Even where a recipe is withheld, every claim remains checkable against dated artifacts, versions, releases, and records, including an explicit statement of what is not disclosed.

## Decision trees and the level of precision

My doctrine stands: decision trees are what make experiments reproducible, and a method becomes a methodology when it is formalized into precise step-by-step procedures. The correction concerns the level at which the steps are precise.

Precision belongs at the decision and checkpoint level, not at the gesture level. A decision tree can be exact — if this state, take this branch; if this failure, stop; if this variant survives selection, carry it forward — without pretending that every brush movement, edit, or micro-choice was a documented step. The steps of a methodology are decisions and changes of state. They are not hand movements.

## What will be documented from now on

For each experiment or series, the record will state: the research question; the project and series it belongs to; the starting materials and state; the phases in order; the critical parameters or parameter ranges; the decision points and the rules applied at them; the deviations and failures; the versions and releases; and the outcome.

Records are kept at checkpoints: at the start, after a committed stage, before a major branch, at failure or abandonment, and at completion.

## What will not be disclosed, and why

Exact recipes are not published: the full parameterization of a singular work, and the tacit micro-decisions that individuate it.

This is a scope statement, not a retreat into secrecy. The recipe is not the transferable part of the method; the method is. The exact recipe of a singular work belongs to that work's individuation, the way a specific performance belongs to its occasion. Publishing methods while declining to publish recipes states the scope of the openness exactly — and an openness with an exactly stated scope is worth more than an unqualified promise that cannot be kept.

## Case study: Noise Fields

The Noise Fields paintings are generated through iterated operations on noisy pixel-value distributions, generation after generation, with selection exercised by my judgment at each stage.

For these works, I disclose: the seed family and the statistical profile of the field; the distribution family; the parameter ranges within which the operations run; the sequence of operation types; and the selection criteria in general terms.

I withhold: the exact seed, and the exact recipe of operations for a given painting.

A reader can therefore run a genuine Noise Fields experiment — generate fields of the same family, vary the parameters within the stated ranges, apply selection, and compare the results as a class — without being handed the means to duplicate a specific painting.

## What readers can legitimately reproduce

The Operation. The basic methods. An effect, or a technique. An experiment of the same design and the same class.

Not a singular artwork, offered as an experiment.

A process, in my practice, is an operation: projects, series, methods, records, failures, versions, and teachable starting procedures. Nothing in this note restores the mystique of the artist. The system is open. The scope of the openness is stated.

— A.G.
