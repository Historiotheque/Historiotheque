**I**
It might be useful if I started by saying a thing or two about this publication, _The Painter’s Almanach._ I have talked about it a good deal before, but I’m not sure anyone has fully understood the import of this important technology, which needs to be accompanied by the _Seasons of The Heart year-long, secular liturgy_ that is sprinkled throughout the _Almanach_ and really brings it to life. Otherwise it would just be dusty, dried calendricals systems (**“w_ith no sense, purpose, or meaning”_**).

The calendars are super important, don’t get me wrong. But what we are talking about here is what I am calling **THE BIRTH OF POLYPHONY** which I argue happened in the 8th and 9th centuries in the Carolingian Renaissance, where people still lived in **_Cyclical Time_**, just before the High Middle Ages, before the clock was really a thing, at least not really (_The Birth of Mechanical Time + The Birth of Linear Chronology + The Great Synchronization_).

I believe that the _Cyclical Time_ of harvests rooted in the soil, the seasons, and the repetitive liturgy of the Church, was fundamentally _monophonic_ and not just in the music at the time, with its new ways of writing musical notation and whatnot, but all across the board. _I think life itself was monophonic. It was a loop; history was merely the repetition of archtetypes. And then, it was polyphonic, and today were are in a hyperreal version of this._

**II
**_The Painter’s Almanach_ was first designed as an actual book, but ended being scattered and distributed all over the web instead. As I said, another project, the _Seasons of The Heart_ (project), is INTEGRAL to this work, which has become a daily practice.

As I have mentioned a hundred times, I am first and foremost a **_Field Artist_**, I work _“in the field”_ collecting data, and then once I return to my art studio + sound design/research laboratory, I turn that data into innovative cultural artifacts. **_It all has to do with the field, with fields._**

Recall that I am _an INTERDISCIPLINARY artist-researcher,_ meaning that I work across Image(s), Sound(s), and Text(s). In my daily practice designed as a year-long liturgical cycle, and based in the fundamentals of _Cyclical Time_, yet adapted to 21st century realities, my work essentially becomes _“Calendar Music” or a “Living Historiography”._ I also suffer from synesthesia-like condition and so I paint sounds and write visual music, and every other combination you can think of. By creating music specifically to accompany the act of painting, for instance, I am creating a _Feedback Loop of Mediums_. Examples_:_

*   **The Painting** records the physical gesture (_History as Space_).
*   **The Music** records the temporal flow (_History as Time_).
*   **The Almanac** acts as the **Chronicler.**

*   I’ve been revisiting the hundreds of pages of notes that I took on the subject of Pablo Picasso, mostly about his _Les Demoiselles d’Avignon_ painting, which many to this day still say that it _“inaugurated modern art”._ I’m not sure, but who am I to argue? I just know that a significant part of any such success is luck but also obviously natural talent, and in this case I think there was a bit of the **MYSTIQUE** that he was shrouded in, perhaps to his dismay. I think though that he and George Braque had genuinely fought against the Romantic view of the artistic genius and the sort of gatekeeping _“authority”_ of artists, but that this was largely ignored over the years after the fact as the mystique took over. Like it or not, though, the mystique likely contributed to his long-lasting success. But anyway originally I think Picasso and Braque had a more collaborative vision of artistic work especially in the early phases of Cubism, seeing their studios operating more as a _laboratory_. More on this later.
*   In any case, I call it **_The Picasso Market._** It’s the art market of the early 20th century in Europe, but also in America. It was global, Cubism was in any case. But mostly it was European. Art historians now speak of _“transnational”_ movements, meaning that they cross many borders and boundaries.
*   I would love it if I could somehow avoid proving Albert-László Barabási’s **First Law of Succes**s right. Realistically, I’m definitely not going to be exhibiting in any high-profile Salons around here anytime soon. But I still hold onto the hope that it’s possible to achieve international acclaim without shelling out for advertising or relying on exhibits in the institutions sitting right at the center of the art world’s complex network of gatekeepers.
*   Barabási argues that he can predict an artist’s long-term success with high accuracy just by looking at their first three exhibitions. Obviously, if you live in a major hub like Brooklyn, have the right connections, and catch a massive wave of luck, making headway is a lot easier. As I said earlier, when I first started out as a youth, I had those grand, starry-eyed aspirations of becoming the next Picasso. While I’ve grown a bit more grounded since then, I still strive for absolute excellence in my work, come what may.
*   I want to believe I can carve out my own path without relentlessly climbing the traditional ladder — even if, deep down, expecting it to just happen feels a bit like magical thinking. But honestly, that hope is what keeps me going these days. The goal, as I see it, is to move from the periphery to the center, and I think the ultimate wild card to get there is disruptive innovation.
*   I’ve been practing my own version of **_The Feldenkrais Method, or “Awareness-Through-Movement”_**. I want to say just for a moment that I document and log everything I do online so that I can leave an audit trail of _“_[_breadcrumbs_](https://github.com/antiface/Documentation/tree/master/METHODS/GeneralWorkflow/Breadcrumbs)_”_ so that people can hold me accountable, but also so I can be easily found or discovered.
