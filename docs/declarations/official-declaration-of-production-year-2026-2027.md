**I. The Process:**
I will start with a short definition and description of what I do exactly as an interdisciplinary artist-researcher and then get to the projects. I have written about this many times before, but as my work is potentially being read by newer and newer audiences,I think it merits a quick reintroduction. Then, as I said, I will go into more detail into the projects.

As an interdisciplinary artist, I work across three main _“Modes of Expression”_, if you will, namely: _Image(s), Sound(s), and Text(s)_. I create works in series in those three disciplines, and the works, and their series, are all interrelated, interconnected, and intersecting, so that there is really only one “artistic expression”, just across three unique modalities.

I believe that this manner of working represents my subjective experience of reality, not perfectly, but it mediates the experience in an expression and captures it through its abstract representations. It’s not perfect because we are only dealing with three separate, superimposed “tracks”, while in reality, in everyday experience phenomenologically, there can be any number of “tracks”.

You can imaging the Image, Sound, and Text tracks similar to how audio tracks are superimposed in most audio editing software, as “layers”:

![“THREE AUDIO TRACKS IN AUDIO EDITOR”. Screenshot by A.G. © 2024. All Rights Reserved.](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*tsIqWouWnOHbqzlVRdB08A.png)

My experience seems to be made up of these kind of geological “strata”. What I am calling the “tracks” are separate and unique. They are unique in their characteristics, traits, qualities, and form. But they all treat of the same underlying phenomenological experience, which I am trying to reproduce as faithfully as I can, through my three disciplines.

As for the rest of the rigorous, comprehensive methodology, as I said, I work in series, in parallel series in the three disciplines (that interrelate and are indexed and documented and make references to one another). I also work _“procedurally”_, if you will, as though the work were being done by an algorithm, as a step by step procedure, only done manually, by hand and not via computer program, _“autonomously”_.

My method in that regard is iterative and experimental, as well as evolutionary. That is, I continuously iterate and reiterate my _“experiments”_ in an evolutionary way so that series and subseries “evolve” towards an ideal that I call in the case of digital visual design, _“visual interestingness”_, i.e. the image is evolved from seed noise until it attains a certain level or quantity of _“visual interestingness”_ or _“VI”_. In the case of music and sound design, I use a similar method. I usually begin with noise, except maybe in my textual work. The idea is that there is a given _“fitness function”_ which decides when one _“generation”_ or _“series”_ ends, giving (terminal) end-states.

I also document the process heavily and publish it online nearly-continuously where it is easily accessible to anyone with a simple web browser. I will revisit the _Process_ in the elaboration of [_The Work-System of A.G., or “ALX”_](https://github.com/antiface/ALX)_,_ in the future, to explain how it is a rigorous, comprehensive, iterative, and experimental design workflow management methodology.

**Index-Reference Resonance:
-** Index-Reference Resonance is an intriguing concept that integrates the principles of memory, neural networks, and non-linear experiential processing. It parallels Rupert Sheldrake’s idea of Morphic Resonance but applies it to cognitive and experiential phenomena, emphasizing the intricate web of connections formed by past experiences. This idea posits that experiences are stored not just as isolated events but as interconnected references within a larger, dynamic index, creating a form of resonance that influences perception and cognition in a non-linear manner.
- At the core of this concept is the Index, a comprehensive, evolving database of all past experiences. It is a mental construct, supported by the neural substrate, that categorizes and organizes memories. The Index is not linear; it does not simply catalog events chronologically but rather organizes them based on associative relevance and experiential significance. Each new experience creates a reference point within the Index, forming nodes in a complex, interconnected network. These references can be linked to similar experiences, such as all past sunsets, and to other seemingly unrelated experiences through associative connections, like a sunset linking to memories of a specific day, emotions, or other sensory experiences.
- Resonance, in this context, is the phenomenon where new experiences activate related references within the Index, creating a non-linear ripple effect. This activation is not merely a recall of past events but a dynamic interplay that can amplify certain aspects of the experience, making it feel more vivid or uncanny.
- Unlike linear memory recall, where events are retrieved in a sequential order, Index-Reference Resonance allows for experiences to be accessed and connected in a non-linear fashion. This means that a current experience can trigger a cascade of associated memories and references that do not follow the original order in which they were experienced. Every new experience is unique, adding a fresh reference to the Index. This new reference does not simply sit in isolation but immediately begins forming connections with existing references, based on multiple factors such as emotional intensity, sensory similarities, contextual relevance, and personal significance.
- The feeling of uncanniness arises when a new experience resonates strongly with a complex web of past references. This can occur due to the activation of a highly interconnected node within the Index. Such experiences might evoke a sense of déjà vu or an intense emotional response because they trigger a rich, multi-layered recall of past experiences.
- The theoretical underpinnings of Index-Reference Resonance can be linked to the idea of Hebbian learning, where “neurons that fire together, wire together.” The formation of references and their interconnections in the Index can be seen as a result of synaptic plasticity and network strengthening over time. Each new experience can thus strengthen or create new pathways in the neural network, contributing to the resonance effect. Associative memory models in cognitive psychology support the idea that memories are stored and retrieved based on associative links. The Index-Reference system expands on this by proposing a more dynamic and non-linear model, suggesting that memories and experiences are not just passively stored but actively interact to influence perception and cognition.
- From a phenomenological perspective, the concept aligns with the idea that experiences are subjective and deeply interconnected. The resonance effect can be seen as a manifestation of the rich, subjective tapestry of personal history and consciousness. The uncanny, as described by Freud, involves the familiar becoming strange. Index-Reference Resonance provides a cognitive mechanism for this phenomenon, where intense resonance with past experiences creates a sense of eerie familiarity.

**The Work-System of A.G., Or “ALX”:
-** The Creative Work-System known as ALX is a carefully developed methodology that integrates artistic expression and research within an interdisciplinary framework. It incorporates three primary forms of expression—images, sound, and text—offering a structured yet experimental approach to creativity. By blending design principles with experimental techniques, ALX enables a deeper exploration of these elements;
- The methodology follows a structured process to manage the creative workflow, ensuring that the entire journey from concept to completion is efficient and coherent. This structured approach emphasizes careful planning and organization, which is critical when navigating the complexities of interdisciplinary practices that combine both art and research;
- A key principle of ALX's creative process is the use of noise as a starting point. The process begins with randomness, which is then modulated in multiple steps to gradually shape the final artistic output. This relentless modulation process transforms the initial chaos into structured, visually compelling compositions, reflecting the artist's ongoing effort to bring order out of disorder;
- ALX is characterized by its distributed and iterative nature. It progresses serially, exploring ambient spaces and employing experimental, procedural, and generative methods to fuel creativity. This multifaceted approach allows for continuous exploration and refinement across various artistic mediums, promoting an evolving and dynamic creative process.
- At its core, ALX transcends the traditional separation between art and research, establishing a deep connection between creative practice and intellectual inquiry. Through the careful documentation and archiving of the images and artistic outputs it generates, ALX contributes not only to the creative arts but also to the broader field of digital image processing. This integration of artistic and research practices fosters new insights, enhancing both the creative and theoretical aspects of the work;
- Ultimately, ALX embodies a significant shift in how art and research intersect, offering a framework that supports exploration, experimentation, and synthesis across multiple disciplines. By harnessing the potential of noise and rigorously applying design workflows, ALX demonstrates the transformative power of interdisciplinary creativity.

**Winters of The Soul:
-** The [“Winters of The Soul”](https://medium.com/the-painters-almanach/winter-of-the-soul-a-journey-through-the-depths-of-creativity-667efecef2f7) explores the cyclical nature of creativity, drawing a parallel between the changing seasons and the phases of artistic life. After periods of intense creative activity, symbolized by autumn, artists often enter a phase of introspection and stillness akin to winter. This time of solitude is not idleness but a crucial pause for reflection, renewal, and the preparation for future creative bursts.
- Inspired by Soren Kierkegaard’s reflections on idleness and Saint John of The Cross’ “dark nights of the soul,” this concept emphasizes the importance of embracing these quiet periods. Kierkegaard suggests that true creativity arises from reflective idleness, while Saint John of The Cross highlights the transformative power of inner darkness leading to renewed light and inspiration. These philosophical insights underscore the value of solitude and introspection in the creative process.
- As artists retreat inward during their soulful winters, they undergo a profound transformation, emerging with renewed purpose and clarity. This period of dormancy mirrors the natural world’s cycle, where the earth rests in winter to prepare for the bountiful growth of spring. The [“Winters of The Soul”](https://medium.com/the-painters-almanach/winter-of-the-soul-a-journey-through-the-depths-of-creativity-667efecef2f7) challenges contemporary notions of constant productivity, advocating for the essential role of stillness and introspection in fostering deep personal and creative growth.

**The Secular Nature of The Seasons of The Heart Liturgy:
**- _Seasons of The Heart_ is a yearly, liturgical Song-Cycle that is similar to the Catholic liturgical cycle except that it is _secular._ Actually, the liturgy includes much more than just a Song-Cycle. It includes images too, paintings and digital art. It also includes a lot of text. In fact, it can include works in ANY medium. What the liturgical calendar does is just give a kind of thematic scaffolding on which you can make art. It’s just a yearly calendar of what is most appropriate, thematically and conceptually;
- It’s important, though, that it be secular and not religious. I have nothing against religion, I’m a practicing Roman Catholic. It’s just that I’m not a religious artist. I try to keep religion and politics out of my art practice in general. It has proven wise to do this over the years.
