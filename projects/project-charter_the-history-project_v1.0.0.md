# Project Charter — The History-Project

**Version:** 1.0.0
**Date opened:** July 15, 2001
**Main run:** July 15, 2001 — October 2004 (ended by a move, not by completion)
**Date of charter:** October 11, 2026
**Status:** Dormant, reopenable — returned to intermittently from around 2006–2007 onward; new History Paintings still made
**Nested in:** None — no parent project at the time; contemporary projects overlapped, none stood above the others
**Modes:** Images, Texts, Sounds — no Code
**Workspace:** A room (about 20 × 20 feet) in a 4½ apartment in Notre-Dame-de-Grâce, Montreal; also on foot and in transit, Montreal ↔ the South Shore

*Charter reconstructed 2026-10-11 from my spoken account of 2026-10-11, my notes, and my online documentation (principally Medium). Uncertain items are marked [uncertain] in the text, never silently filled.*

## 1. Intention

The History-Project was an interdisciplinary art-research project: images, sounds, and words — essays, fiction and non-fiction, everything.

To paint the concept — or concepts — of History, and to test whether that is even possible: whether a concept can be communicated through visual means, as people claim a visual language can. The starting position was the null hypothesis: that there is no such visual language, and a concept cannot be communicated unambiguously by painting.

Around that test: representation and the limits of representation; knowledge and the archaeology of knowledge; geological formations; physical memory and three-dimensional memory. To be a witness to History as it was being made in the present, in Montreal, in a period that began just before September 11, 2001.

It was a representation of History: of being in one's time, one's era — what it was like to live in 2001, 2002, at that time — and trying to understand what it had been like for the Cubists and the other artists and thinkers of the early 20th century, in their times.

The main research mixed art / history / philosophy: art history, art historiography, the philosophy of art, the philosophy of history. Quebec and Canadian history belonged to it: the seigneurial system, the Habitant, survivance across history — land laid out in long strips perpendicular to the rivers, each strip fronting the water and running back from it; the roads parallel to the river are the rangs, the roads running perpendicular, climbing inland, are the montées, another rang lying further back after one depth of strips.

## 2. Objectives

Paint a new concept of History with each work, the series itself carrying the research. Develop the concepts behind the paintings in parallel — in the fiction, the poetry, the music, and the philosophical research — all of it interconnected: strategic intermedia.

Find, through texture if not through language, a means of representing History: the wear and tear of History, the patina of time, the passage and weathering of the elements — the surface textures of old stone, of wood, studied closely.

## 3. Realisations

- **The History-Paintings** — hundreds of paintings; mostly abstract, in the style I named History-Painting; also figurative work, many self-portraits.
- **Drawings** — a great many, in charcoal, pastel, pencil, and markers.
- **The Legal Tender Project** — a sub-project within the paintings, on currency, imitating the aesthetics of paper money.
- **The novel *The History-Project*** — about a group of public intellectuals who come together to work on a project funded by the government in Quebec: to investigate why there was a higher incidence of depression and anxiety disorders in Quebec than in the rest of Canada. A premise invented to give them a reason to have a history project — an interdisciplinary art-research project like my own. Its figures: Alphonse Lemoyne, Painter A., a schizophrenic painter; J.G. Dufray, sound designer, radio DJ, and composer; Dr. Evelyn, philosopher, who worked on habitance; and Dr. Viktor Kropotkin, a retired psychiatrist from Saint Petersburg, inventor of historiotherapeusis — historical therapy, Historiotherapy. Among its characters also: the Messenger-Boy — the Boy from the Cemetery — who took part in experiments with Dr. Kropotkin; and the Baron, figured as a seigneurial lord. Dr. Kropotkin developed the theory he called the Baron Complex, having to do with the Baron. Mental illness figured heavily in the project, in the Doctor's experiments and beyond. In this novel I invented what I call the novelistic phenomenology — the phenomenology of the novel. The novel carried a lot of metafiction: the fiction about itself, characters becoming conscious and aware of themselves as fictional characters trapped in a fiction, and putting the Author on trial.
- **The novelistic writings** — short fiction and poetry; the writing gathering into *Crackland and The Crackland Journals* (about 1000 pages of fragments, essays, and mostly fictional writing), with a physical cosmology of lands — Crackland, St-Elsewhere, and the Dream Assembly, the Dream Assembly coming from my Exhibition / Tonal Cinema novellas.
- **The Diary of a Self-Portrait.**
- **The philosophical and art-historical writing** — hundreds of pages of philosophical essays on Picasso, Cubism, Surrealism, and modernism, on art history itself, and on the philosophy of History.
- **Concept albums of songs** — 10 to 12 albums, maybe 40 songs apiece, almost 400 songs or more, the subjects tied to the fiction (Father Time, the Boy from the Cemetery, Mr. Buxby). Names remembered in part: *For Lent* No. 1 and No. 2, *Time and History*.
- **Exhibitions:** a cultural centre in Côte-des-Neiges [institution name not remembered]; the Musée des beaux-arts de Mont-Saint-Hilaire.
- Most of the works are held in my physical archives.

## 4. Parameters

Scope: open. Nothing was excluded; there was no deliberate out-of-scope. The fiction and the research covered everything I brought to them.

Constraints: I had just moved and had no canvases — paintings were made in varied sizes mostly on cardboard, boxes taken apart with a box cutter. I worked a lot on cardboard.

The main run ended in October 2004 [exact day not recorded] because I moved away — not because the project finished.

## 5. Means and Temporal Logic

Materials: mixed media — acrylic with markers, collage — and oil paint; cardboard; charcoal, pastel, pencil, and markers for the drawings. Found old paper: accounts and ledgers books, receipts, material of the 1930s or so [age uncertain], personally identifiable information removed by me, worked into collages.

Techniques, my own: Axes, Conduits, and Templates; the Templates of History, the Figures of History; my own painting style, worked in layers, treatments applied to the surface. Across all media: cut-up, collage, montage.

In sound: guitar and voice in my own style — a melody sung, then sung again an octave higher; two guitars at times; percussion at times — plus sound-design work and MIDI music on the computer.

Dream material drawn from a dream log / dream journal I kept; I dreamed about the project itself.

An attempt at textbook art: an abstract handbook imitated from a textbook, on the model of a Surrealist textbook — read like a physics textbook, but carrying an allegorical meaning.

Study at the time: symbolic logic, axiomatic set theory, calculus, mathematics generally, a physics textbook; Bertrand Russell, *The Principles of Mathematics*.

Temporal logic: daily work, always working — in quick bursts, and at times for hours and hours, oil-painting sessions sometimes 14 hours at a time — under my serial and iterative evolutionary methodology, which started here. Each work a continuation of the series, exploring a new concept of History; series spawning sub-series. After the main run, dormancy and reopenings, not closure.

I walked a lot at the time, was working in Montreal, and travelled often down to the South Shore to visit family and friends.

## 6. Relations and Record

Others: the project was uniquely my own. I also worked at times with other artists — in NDG, with old friends, on the South Shore and in Otterburn Park — doing art together around it. Witnesses: the friend I lived with at the time, and a few other friends.

Draws-on, at the time: Walter Benjamin, Michel Foucault, Gilles Deleuze and Guattari, Martin Heidegger, Edmund Husserl, Jean-Paul Sartre, Søren Kierkegaard, Gabriel Marcel, Immanuel Kant (the Critiques), Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel (*Science of Logic*), William S. Burroughs, Ferdinand de Saussure (semiotics, linguistics); Cubism — everything I could read about it, over 100 sources, books and essays from academic journals I had access to at the time; film theory; philosophy of history; Surrealism; Franz Kafka, André Gide (*Les Faux-Monnayeurs*), Fyodor Dostoevsky, classic literature; Canadian and Quebec history — the seigneurial system and the land distribution of the early French colony.

Overlaps: *The Exhibition and Tonal Cinema* (before it, overlapping, to around 2002).

Descendants: *Writing-Without-Writing* (around 2006, the next project proper, an attempt at asemic writing); *The Archives-Project* (begun around 2011, a direct descendant).

Record: the project is documented in my notes and online, principally on Medium. The History-Function, H(x), is a later formalization of this work and forms no part of the project as it stood in 2001–2004.

Standing assessment, mine: a concept cannot be communicated directly, as language communicates — painting lacks the syntax, the recursion by which language extends itself indefinitely. What was achieved was achieved through texture.
