## What I did

Fifteen days, and the window has two centres and a gap. The first
centre is a harvest: the Valéry study, which the last log left at
eleven registered passages, was carried through all six of its
sections and compiled into a single coded corpus of 1,311 entries —
the coordinates method at full scale, with its schema stress-tested
and one of its own claims caught and corrected in the open. Around
it, a week of building and drafting: the Refcards app rebuilt and
then run; two theory documents drafted, one of them by sorting
outside elaborations and setting aside what was not mine; a ruling
sheet that turns a month-old judgment into four checkboxes; a cover
for the Experimental Novel. The second centre came at the end, and
it was forced on the practice: on the evening of November 19 I was
locked out of my own accounts over a suspicious-activity flag, and
because my sign-in to the Archivillus System runs through the
suspended account, the lock reached the work system itself. I
checked everything with my own eyes and found nothing that was not
my own activity; the account was sound; but the full return took
days, and two of them left no records at all. The answer the
practice made to that lesson is the window's last work: an
Operational Working Model of this system — what it is, how it
operates, what it would take for the work to continue if access to
any outside service it leans on were lost again. A dependency you
do not control is a risk you have already taken; the model is the
risk, answered in writing.

Underneath the dated work, the days kept their fabric, and it
belongs in this introduction because it does not belong to any one
day: a morning walk every day — my field expeditions, at the
day's first light. Writing every day in a new social note-taking
app, a great deal of it. Work on the novel, pressed on with
deliberately. Hours and hours of musical composition — much more
than I had composed in a long time. And the library: a soundproof
booth there where I do hours of research every day, spending as
much time as possible outside my apartment. This is the winter
direction I set for myself — field, library, music, the novel —
lived daily, and it is exactly how the disconnected days were
spent when they came: expeditions in the morning, the library's
booth, the music. The Archivillus System was locked; the practice
was not.

computer, under my own hand — can be stopped cold by that service,
on suspicion alone, with the appeal measured in days. That is a

## 2026-11-23 — workspace=[lab] × session_type=[research, designing, discourse] — the Operational Working Model

ΔW temp = lucid, tightening, conclusive

The return completed itself in the morning: my professional
artist Page found standing deactivated — not deleted, its
contents intact, recoverable in one click. The Historiotheque
Page, the studio's home, stood unaffected; it had never been
deactivated. I reactivated the artist Page the same day, once the
checking was done: after an episode like this one, the verifying
comes first and the click second.

Then the window's last work, and its largest. A channel was
founded for one purpose: to build an Operational Working Model of
the Archivillus System — of my work, workflow, and workspace as
they actually operate. The constraints I set were strict:
everything within this system alone, no connectors to my other
accounts, no web search, no speculation — the model grounded in
the files I already hold. The research I brought to it — my own
notes and the outside elaborations I have been reading on agentic
working models — served to explain the concept; the model itself
was compiled from the practice's own record.

Three deliverables, in one day. First, the compilation: the model
in four parts — operating principles and rules; workspace and
project architecture; conceptual frameworks and schemas; logging
and documentation standards — every section tagged as in the files
or a gap in the files, with a gaps list of 23 items naming exactly
what is incomplete, outdated, or missing. Second, the inventory:
every file in scope listed and compared against the compilation —
1,918 files, of which the compilation had read about 30 in full.
The inventory is the compilation's adversarial review, and it
earned the name: the model is a faithful summary of those 30
files; it is not a summary of the whole system, and now the
difference is proved, file by file — three of the compilation's
gap-claims did not survive it. Third, the philosophical concept:
the theory written as a generalized theoretical document, some
10,000 words in eleven sections — naming no service, no platform,
and none of the research documents, per my rule; the Archivillus
case history retained in it by my ruling, as a case, together with
what the inventory proved. A second version the same day resolved
its one open question, on my clarification, and the clarification
is doctrine, so it is stated here in full: when I call the
Switchboard decisionless, I do not mean there are no decisions —
I mean I do not plan the day's sequence of tasks in advance.
Proximity is itself one of the only actual decision rules: take
whatever is closest or nearest at hand. And there is no conflict
with the stack: the studio surface has a geological structure —
actual stacks, actual layers — so what is nearest at hand is
physically the top of a stack, and taking it is a stack-pop by the
nature of the space. Access is priced, besides: what is buried
costs — moving stacks, rummaging through layers — and the deeper
the layer, the steeper the cost; taking the top is not costless
either, physical actions are still required, but its cost is
negligible by comparison. The gradient never reaches zero.

Why the model exists, stated as plainly as the lockout taught it:
an Operational Working Model is the practice written down well
enough that the practice does not live inside any one service.
If access to an agentic system I rely on is lost — suspended,
discontinued, priced away, gone — the model is what a successor
operator, human or machine, runs from: the rules, the schemas, the
architecture, the standards, all on my own ground. The problem I
have named Agentic Failure stands at its centre: the agentic
system itself does not hold such a model of the practice it
serves; it improvises one, session by session, and calls the
improvisation memory. The model is the correction — mine, written,
and mine to keep.
