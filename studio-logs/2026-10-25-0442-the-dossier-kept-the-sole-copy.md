## — workspace=[lab] × session_type=[research] — precursor archaeology: the coinages set right
ΔW temp = exact, lucid, methodical

I commissioned an archaeology of the genetic-criticism discourse,
keyed to my research questions, and it was delivered that day. It
verifies the coinage of avant-texte: Jean Bellemin-Noël, *Le texte
et l'avant-texte* (1972). And it corrects the analysis filed the
night before: exogenesis and endogenesis were not coined by Dirk
Van Hulle — they are Raymonde Debray Genette's (*Essais de critique
génétique*, 1979), given systematic definitions by Pierre-Marc de
Biasi (1998); Van Hulle completed the triad with epigenesis (2014),
its assembler, not the pair's coiner. The
three terms are research background here, not my formal vocabulary;
where they appear in my theory documents, the corrected chain is
the one to credit. The analysis's credit line needs repair, and the
repair waits on my ruling. The report also
finds Ferrer's exact term for the draft-as-protocol —
"protocole opératoire" — and gives the discipline's founding as a
sequence, not a year.

## — workspace=[lab] × session_type=[research] — the landscape scan: no precedent located
ΔW temp = lucid, conclusive, tightening

The second commissioned report asked whether anyone else works as
the Experimental Novel works: version control as the foundation,
structure, and inspiration of an abstract novel-as-a-system,
published as a live, logged dossier on the web. Tested against that
six-point conjunction, no precedent was located, and no candidate
meets more than three points at once. The closest: Flashbake (Thomas
Gideon's tool, adopted and publicized by Cory Doctorow, 2009 — real
git composition, in private repositories, the versioning never
entering the novels); Nick Montfort's Taroko Gorge and its fork
lineage (version-descent as form — but nothing merges, and it is a
poem engine, not a novel); Brandon Sanderson's Warbreaker drafts,
posted as written (the strongest dossier precedent — no version
control, no system); Joshua Cohen's PCKWCK, a novel typed live on
the web in 2015 and withdrawn to print; the Oulipo's protocols,
published as literature. What I may not claim: writing in
git, publishing drafts as written, live composition, version-descent
as form, protocols as literature. What the reports offer as the
claimable novelty, precisely: a novel's version control that is at
once its compositional foundation, its structural model, and its
public form — the dossier génétique kept prospectively by the
author, in commit-citing, movement-only logs.

choose to take it up when I open it.

## — workspace=[lab] × session_type=[discourse] — context can be added backwards
ΔW temp = methodical, lucid, settling

A principle I stated in the LOGGING channel, emphatically: the
no-backfilling rule forbids silently revising a record; it does not
forbid reconstruction. Context can always be added backwards, marked
as reconstruction — a sparse record, a handful of minimal variables
and nodes, can be enriched later, honestly. An idea I threw out
with it, not adopted: collect at least a minimal trace for every
work — a title, a date even approximate, photographs of its states
— because from those nodes the rest could be rebuilt. The summer
series below are the principle's first exercise.

## — workspace=[lab] × session_type=[designing, discourse] — capture away from the workstation
ΔW temp = tightening, methodical, exact

The problem stated plainly: work done away from the terminal —
painting in the studio, field recordings and video in the field —
often goes unlogged, because logging at a terminal mid-work is
impractical; yet it may be the most important data in the practice,
and forgetting to report it is a failure of the Art Operation that
needs a remedy. Decided that morning: narration into the field
recorder in the imperative mood is my phenomenological-description
form going forward; when the recorder fails, dictation from memory,
logged verbatim in the imperative and marked as my report — the wash
session above is its first instance. Proposed, and standing as
proposals until I rule: the remedy target is real-time capture, not
real-time logging — event time and ingestion time kept separate, as
the chain-of-custody rule already demands; one spoken imperative as
one atomic unit of work; the audio file itself standing as a
sound-stream record beside the text. And the design constraint that
governs all of it: any method that requires stopping the art to feed
the log fails by definition — the practice bends to the studio and
the field, never the reverse.

## 2026-10-23 — workspace=[lab] × session_type=[discourse, research] — dating thirty years of paintings
ΔW temp = methodical, exact, tightening

A methodological problem I posed: I have kept almost all the
paintings and drawings of thirty years — acrylic, charcoal, pencil,
ink, pastels, oil — but titles and dates were not recorded
systematically when the works were made. Many backs are inscribed,
and my signature carried the date from around 2004 onward; many
works were photographed at some point, so photo timestamps survive
as evidence. The question: date a work by its photo date, with the
creation date unknown — or reconstruct from mentions in the writings
and logs? The protocol advised, and not yet adopted: never substitute
the photo date for the creation date; keep two dates apart — the
creation date as a range, with its basis and certainty stated, and
the photo date recorded as the date documented; photograph the back
in the same pass as the front; one inventory row per work, under a
stable identifier; enriched entries marked as reconstruction; and,
proposed but not adopted, a two-minute rule going forward — title
it, sign and date the back, one row. The offer to draft it as a standing
procedure stands open.
