## 2026-10-15 11:31 → 12:34 — workspace=[lab] × session_type=[discourse, research] — characterology: rulings, a statement, a prior-art pass
ΔW temp = exact, tightening, methodical

I ruled on the expansion's four open points: the knot may be one core or
a sequence (kept open in my terms); the space of problems is not declared
finite; transcendence is a possible state in the sequence, not a
terminal one; and the proposed ethical clause — that every entry must
carry the problem's possible transcendence — is NOT adopted as a
requirement. The rulings were folded into a consolidated statement,
delivered in the channel: my core doctrine, with the extrapolations I
have not ruled on carried separately and marked as proposals. Two
attribution checks followed at my asking: the Nietzsche thread (the
*Gay Science* aphorisms) is kept, reworded as my interpretation, never as
a definition Nietzsche states; and my use of Sartre's "hell is other
people" was checked against the scholarship and confirmed, with two
precisions — the Other's look is another person stating your
character-problem for you and fixing it as an essence. I adopted Sartre's
fundamental project as a component of the theory, not merely a precursor,
and added that characters in the theory are like equations in Salmon's
conception — problem-formulations in signs, to be worked, not symbols to
be decoded. Then, at my instruction and with the cost acknowledged, a
deeper bibliographic pass ran: the term *characterology* is not my
coinage (Bahnsen, 1867; the German and French schools after him classify
by traits — mine classifies by problems, and the "abstract" and the
problem-content are the differentiators); the closest precursor is
Kundera — a character as an experimental self, and making a character
alive as getting to the bottom of his existential problem; Souriau stands
as the hinge find. The recommendation I accepted: cite the precursors
openly, and let the originality rest on the synthesis and the schema.
