The question came from seeing a CHANGELOG.md at the root of someone else's repository: should the Historiotheque repo have one? Deliberated with Archivillus; the answer was yes, in the Keep a Changelog form — hand-written, for human readers, one entry per published version, versions assigned at publication as always. Two decisions carry the weight. First, the two lineages: the Medium releases are releases of the practice itself (2.0.0 to 5.1.0), and the GitHub repositories carry their own version numbers with their release notes living beside them — the two are never merged. Second, the pre-GitHub releases were announcements of a documented practice, not a running system; the changelog says so plainly. The light draft runs 2.0.0 (early May 2016) to 5.1.0 (2026-07-03), with an Unreleased section holding the GitHub era. A discovery along the way: my own 5.1.0 release already ends with a CHANGELOG section in this exact format — the root file collects a habit I already have. Sparse entries stay sparse: a changelog records changes, not content, and a short entry is the honest record when few things changed. Pushed to the repo root tonight.
