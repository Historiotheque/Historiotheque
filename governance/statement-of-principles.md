# Statement of Principles — The Art Operation @ The Historiotheque

*First draft, 2026-09-24. These are vows, not aspirations — they describe how the work is actually done, and where it falls short, the record says so.*

**1. Intellectual honesty.** I record what happened, including the blunders, the friction, and the days the machine didn't start. A record that only contains successes is a brochure, not an archive.

**2. Transparency — in the light.** Everything done out in the open. No secrecy, no hidden drafts of the record. I have nothing to hide, and the practice is built to show it.

**3. Accountability.** I operate as if a stranger has the right to verify every claim — because one day, a stranger will. The audit trail tells the story; my job is to leave a trail worth auditing.

**4. Reproducibility.** Young artists should be able to reproduce my experiments. That means the *procedure* is reproducible, not the artwork: document the protocol so completely that someone else can re-run it and get *their* result. Same method, honestly run — necessarily singular outcome.

**5. Accessibility.** The record must be retrievable by someone who never met me: plain formats, public repositories, stable identifiers, timestamps on everything. An archive nobody can find is a diary, not an instrument.

**6. Contemporaneousness, honestly adapted.** In a dynamic practice, capturing everything at the very moment is next to impossible — so I capture at the nearest capture point, timestamp the capture, and mark every reconstruction as a reconstruction. Annotate, never backdate.

**7. Reconstruction as a discipline.** I reconstruct the timeline through continuous review and retrospectives. Where memory was compressed, I say so: some artifacts in the record are compression artifacts — I was making do with what I had, and I am far from perfect. The record converges on the truth through review, not through perfection at capture. What was fact and what was artifice, the audit trail will show.

*Principles 8–12 adopted 2026-09-24.*

**8. Discretion.** I publish what serves the work and withhold what doesn't — other people's private lives, confidences shared in trust, anything that could endanger someone. Honesty is not exposure. The witness decides what the record needs; the rest stays out, marked as withheld.

**9. Respect.** I treat every person who appears in the record — collaborator, subject, stranger, critic — as someone who came to understand, not as material. No one is used.

**10. Autonomy.** The operation answers to no institution, no funder, no platform. I keep the independence of the work by keeping the independence of the worker: no obligations that can later be called in against the record.

**11. Impartiality.** Distance is what makes the audit honest. I judge the work — mine and others' — without favor and without a stake in the outcome. Disinterestedness is a working condition, not a virtue I claim to possess.

**12. Independence.** Never a dependent of the State. No subsidies for the work, no grants that buy a say in it — the gift that is a curse, the White Elephant: answering to bureaucrats and technocrats who do not have your interests at heart. Exception, stated plainly: disability-related state support is not dependence in this sense. Disability rights and advocacy is a principle of this operation, and rules have exceptions by definition.

*For review and revision by the Chief Art Operator.*
