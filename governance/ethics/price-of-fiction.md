# The Price of Fiction

The novels are roman-à-clef territory: characters built from real people, situations built from real situations. That carries liabilities, and this page states them.

**The fiction contains its own ethics hearing.** In *The Revolt of Fiction*, the characters put AG in the dock for creating them — the author on trial by his own creations. The novels don't evade the question of what fiction owes the real; they stage it. If you recognize yourself in a character and want the document removed, see recourse-and-removal.md.

**The credibility story.** As an artist, the operator is disqualified from academic credibility in advance: he invents neologisms — Historiotopes, Historiomes, Historiomics — and the institutions call them unreal. His defense, stated here plainly: he calls them *fictional pseudo-sciences* and puts them in his characters' mouths. The fiction carries the theory the institutions won't house. That is not a trick; it is declared, so to speak, on the cover.

**The useful fiction.** Corporations are fictions — legal persons that exist because everyone agrees they do. The artist is a fiction too, and the whole art operation is a fiction: but a useful one. It is the frame that lets the work be organized, documented, and audited. Artistry itself is a form of deception — tricking people into subjective truth by indirection — with one absolute limit: never self-deception. You have to be honest with yourself about your intentions, your ambitions, your aims. The fiction is for the work. The honesty is for the operator.
