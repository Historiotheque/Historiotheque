# Declaration Warnings

The warnings given in the Official Declarations, in the Art-Ops-In-A-Box Manual (README and chapter 9), and spoken in the video discourses — collected here, each with an interpretation key: what it means, and what it does NOT mean.

## The core line

**"A word of caution, not a prohibition — I'm not saying don't do it."**

- What it means: the operation documents real procedures with real risks; the reader is an adult and will decide for themselves.
- What it does NOT mean: that the risks aren't real, or that the operator endorses unsupervised experimentation.

## The distinctions

**Reproduce the effect, not the artwork.** Technique is knowledge; the artwork is property. Learn the procedure, run it honestly, get *your* result. Copying the artwork is not research — it's forgery with extra steps.

**Supervised practice, not unsupervised experimentation.** The procedures were developed under the operator's own supervision — thirty years of it. A reader working alone is unsupervised by definition; the warning asks them to find supervision: a teacher, a practitioner, a community — someone who has done it before.

**Readiness ("not yet"), not prohibition ("never").** Some procedures require a prepared operator. "Not yet" is a statement about readiness, not a ban. Come back when you're ready; the record will still be here.

## The burnout rationale

The warnings also guard against the work consuming the worker. The solo-developer pattern: unsustainable bursts, then near-burnout — about five times a year, by the operator's count — then shutdown "until further notice," then rest, then relaunch.

The shutdown is a safety valve and part of the design, not a failure. The Official Releases version it the way software versions downtime: the operation scales *down to zero* and back up. A solo operation that can't scale down breaks its operator. The warning says: this work will try to burn you out. Pace yourself. The shutdown is the instrument — use it before you need it.

## The first rule of the art operation

**This might fail drastically — catastrophic failure.** It might ruin your family, your reputation. People will be adversarial: they will judge the work, call you crazy, call you a propagandist, call you a shill. The warnings don't prevent this. They only make sure no one walks in blind.
