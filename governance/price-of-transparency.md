# The Price of Transparency

(Working title. Alternatives under consideration: the price of accountability, the price of being forthcoming.)

Working in the light costs. This page is the bill, stated in advance.

**Prior-publication lockout.** Journals that require unpublished work will not take work that was documented in the open first. Transparency closes some doors. They stay closed.

**Exposing methods learned from other artists.** Documenting your procedure honestly means documenting what you learned from others — including living artists who never published their methods. The operation names what it can and anonymizes what it must (see ethics/source-protection.md), but the tension doesn't resolve.

**Market-disruption liability.** Secrecy is the foundation of the art market as currently built: scarcity of information, mystique of process, the master's untold methods. An operation that publishes its procedures is defecting from that economy, and the economy notices. Accusations get absorbed; no names are named here.

**The visibility cycle.** Iconophilia, then iconophobia, then iconoclasm: they love you, then they fear you, then they destroy you. This is the cost of being seen, and being seen is the price of working in the light. Your art, if it persists, will see all sorts of seasons — and so will you.

**The isolation cost.** The work requires isolation — to a larger or smaller extent, but always. You will need to decide: is it worth the sacrifice to your family and friends, to live in a kind of isolation from the world? This folder doesn't answer that. It only records that the question is real, and that the cost is paid whether or not it's acknowledged.

None of this is an argument against transparency. It's the accounting. We're settling accounts — this is what the light costs, and the operation pays it with its eyes open.
